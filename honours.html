002 : University of Pretoria — BHons CS

Currently completing a Bachelor of Honours in Computer Science at the University of Pretoria, building on my BSc IT from NWU with a deep focus on artificial intelligence, machine learning and research methodology. The programme requires a minimum of 120 credits of postgraduate coursework and culminates in an independent research dissertation supervised by a faculty member. Admission requires a 60% average over all third-year Computer Science modules.

Modules

Research Methodology & Project

A two-part compulsory module. The first semester covers research best practices: designing rigorous studies, conducting literature reviews and structuring academic writing. The project component then applies these methods to produce an original technical research report — which can be a new piece of software, a model, an algorithm, or a theoretical contribution — in one of the department's active research areas.

Artificial Intelligence I
Genetic Programming & Grammatical Evolution

Focuses on Evolutionary Computation and Swarm Intelligence as two Computational Intelligence paradigms. Covers genetic algorithms, genetic programming, grammatical evolution, evolutionary strategies, differential evolution and co-evolution — all modelling Darwinian evolution. Also explores swarm-based optimisers such as ant colony optimisation and particle swarm optimisation, applied to multi-objective and dynamic complex problem spaces.

Artificial Intelligence II
Neural Networks

Covers three Computational Intelligence paradigms. Artificial Neural Networks are studied in depth — including supervised, unsupervised and reinforcement learning, and the factors that govern network performance. Artificial Immune Systems model the human biological immune system (negative selection, clonal selection, network and danger theory). Fuzzy Systems introduce reasoning under uncertainty using fuzzy logic and rough sets.

Software Engineering I

A comprehensive study of contemporary software engineering theory and practice, addressing the challenges of scale, complexity and urgency in real-world systems. Covers design, development, deployment and ongoing operation of software, and shows how standardisation and reuse strategies lead to software that is predictable, repeatable, robust and aligned with user requirements.

Data Mining

Covers the full knowledge-discovery pipeline from raw data to actionable insight. Topics include process models (CRISP-DM and SEMMA), exploratory data analysis, dimensionality reduction via PCA and feature selection, descriptive modelling (clustering and association rules), and predictive modelling using decision trees, k-NN, Naive Bayes, neural networks and ensemble methods. Text and image mining are also introduced.

Natural Language Processing

Introduces students to data and information analytics through mining both structured and unstructured text data. Students apply generative AI techniques to extract insights from data and use novel methods to collect and analyse personal and public web postings through big data case studies. The module develops knowledge of core NLP algorithms — covering information extraction, machine translation, text summarisation and question answering — and the underlying algorithmic and linguistic foundations needed to work with real-world textual data.

Image Processing

A special-topics module in which the Department of Computer Science presents current and advanced research themes. In the current year the topic is digital image processing: covering the mathematical and algorithmic foundations of acquiring, transforming and analysing images. Topics include spatial-domain and frequency-domain filtering (Fourier transforms), edge detection, morphological operations, image segmentation and feature extraction — the core building blocks for computer vision systems applied in fields from medical imaging to autonomous vehicles.
